# 037 — Plan

Spec: [`specs/037.close-price-ladders-repeat-the-zero-price-laundering.md`](../specs/037.close-price-ladders-repeat-the-zero-price-laundering.md)

## Build order

1. Extract spec `035`'s market-deviation test into one module-level pure
   predicate, `closeGivesValueAway(orderPrice, marketPrice, isLong)`, and point
   the existing single-order check at it. No behaviour change — this is the
   precondition for §4.3 covering three call sites without three copies of the
   rule.
2. Take-profit ladder: add the §4.1 zero guard on the rung's own price, before
   and after the second rounding, setting a `ladderPriceUnusable` flag; refuse
   the whole ladder after the rungs are collected.
3. Take-profit ladder: replace the notional-floor block's price rewrite with a
   quantity raise (§4.2).
4. Stop-loss ladder: the same two changes.
5. Add the §4.3 sweep over `tpOrders` immediately before `getTPOrder` returns.

## Contract decisions the spec left open

- **Refuse the ladder, not the rung.** The spec requires a rung that cannot be
  priced to refuse "the whole ladder"; the implementation returns `[]` from
  `getTPOrder`, matching what spec `035` already does for the single order, so
  callers see one consistent "nothing to place" answer.
- **Rounding direction at the floor.** The old block rounded the replacement
  quantity *down*, which is precisely why it needed the price rewrite: rounding
  down could leave the notional a hair under the floor. Rounding *up* clears
  the floor by construction and removes the reason the price rewrite existed.
- **Where §4.3 runs.** Kept the existing mid-function check (so the `aggregate`
  early return keeps today's behaviour) and added a sweep at the single exit.
  The single-order path is therefore checked twice, on the same value, by the
  same predicate — idempotent, and cheaper than restructuring the returns.
- **Threshold unchanged.** `MAX_CLOSE_PRICE_DEVIATION` stays at 10x. This
  change extends an already-shipped policy to two more branches; re-tuning it
  is a separate decision.

## JIT extraction tier

**Tier 1** on the deviation predicate only: pulled into its own named,
module-level pure function, tested through the call sites rather than directly.
Tier 2+ is not justified — the surrounding method is a 900-line block in a
23k-line legacy module, and the radius rule limits this touch to the three
price derivations actually on the causal path. The ladder blocks themselves are
left in place, guarded, rather than extracted.

## Blast radius

1. **Other call sites of the changed code.** `getTPOrder` has 7 call sites in
   `dcaHelper.ts`. Two (`aggregate: true`) return before the ladder blocks and
   before the new sweep — unchanged. The rest reach the ladders only when
   `useMultiTp`/`useMultiSl` is set. `closeGivesValueAway` is new; its only
   consumers are the two checks inside `getTPOrder`.
2. **`core/` submodule.** Yes — this is `main-app-sh`, shared and deployed
   platform-wide (cloud and self-hosted). The parent's pointer needs bumping at
   ship time. `getTPOrder` is DCA/combo bot-engine internal: it is not a
   GraphQL, REST, socket.io or queue shape, and no cross-service contract in
   the Danger List is touched.
3. **Owned/emitted shapes.** None. No schema, event name, queue payload or
   shared collection field changes. The only externally visible change is that
   an order which would have been mispriced is now not placed, and a named
   error is recorded instead.
4. **Other tests on this path.** `tpPriceRefusal` (spec 035), `tpNanRefusal`
   (023), `nanLadderRefusal` (025), `tpSizingFromPosition`, `tpFees`,
   `partialTp`, `multiTpCompletion`, `tpCoverageReconcile` — all must stay
   green. Full suite: 680 passing.
