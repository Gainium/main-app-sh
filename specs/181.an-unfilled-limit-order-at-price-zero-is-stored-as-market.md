# 181 — An unfilled limit order reported at price 0 is stored as MARKET

## 1. Definition

### 1.1 — expected

When the bot places an order, it classifies it from the venue's reply as
MARKET or LIMIT (`placedOrderType`, `src/bot/placedOrderType.ts`). A
requested LIMIT order the venue reports with nothing executed and a price of
`0` has not traded, and is classified LIMIT.

### 1.2 — actual / unchanged

The reply's price was compared with the requested price, and any difference
classified the order MARKET. A venue that reports a resting order's (still
empty) average price as its price answers `"0"`, which differs from every
real price, so every limit order it accepted was stored as MARKET — and so
skipped by everything that looks for resting LIMIT orders (missed-fill
recovery, the dashboard, health checks).

Unchanged: a MARKET type or any fills is MARKET; a non-zero price other than
the requested one is MARKET; price `0` with something executed, or on a
requested MARKET order, is MARKET; an average fill price
(`cummulativeQuoteQty / executedQty`) other than the requested price is
MARKET. The classification is moved verbatim into a pure function; the only
behavioural change is the §1.1 guard.

## 2. Evidence

Resting orders on classic Bitget spot accounts are stored as
`type: MARKET`, `executedQty: 0`, with `price` equal to the requested price,
written by the placement-response path. With no fills and nothing executed,
only a returned price of exactly `"0"` reaches MARKET and then falls back to
the requested price. The connector is fixed to report the limit price
(exchange-connector core spec 038); this guard keeps any venue that still
answers `0` from flipping a resting order.

## 3. Assumption

A reply price of 0 with nothing executed carries no information about how
the order traded. Hypothesis, supported by the evidence above.
