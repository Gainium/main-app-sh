# 142 — Trailing ignores price ticks older than the deal's latest fill

Bug spec.

## §1 Definition

- §1.1 **Expected.** A DCA (or combo) deal's trailing take profit arms, and its
  trailing level moves, only on a price that is at least as recent as the
  deal's latest fill. A fill is a trade the venue really executed at its
  `updateTime`, so a price observed before that moment says nothing about where
  the market is after it.
- §1.2 **Actual.** `checkTrailing` acts on whatever `getLastStreamData()` holds.
  When the price feed lags (ticks delivered seconds after the exchange stamped
  them), a fill can land first and move the deal's average, and therefore its
  take-profit line, below the lagging price. The next lagging tick then reads
  as "price above the take profit": the trail arms, cancels the deal's
  remaining safety orders, and the next lagging tick under the armed level
  fires the trailing close. The market close fills at the real price, below
  the average, so the deal closes at a loss with close reason "take profit".
- §1.3 **Unchanged.** A tick whose exchange time is at or after the deal's
  latest fill is handled exactly as today. Trailing take profit still closes by
  market. A deal with no fill recorded in this process (for example, right
  after a worker restart) behaves as today. So does a tick without a usable
  timestamp.

## §2 Evidence

- §2.1 Worker log for one long, 25x Bybit USDT-perp deal during a fast drop.
  These are the safety-order fills in exchange time, each logged by the bot
  within about 150 ms of `updateTime`:
  0.17131 (:08.4), 0.16934 (:15.7), 0.16747 (:20.6), 0.16569 (:22.1),
  0.16399 (:22.3), 0.16239 (:25.097).
- §2.2 The same worker's trailing lines, in wall time:
  `:25.273 Trailing: will cancel 8 orders … level: 0.1705293, price: 0.1707`,
  then `:25.909 Trailing trigger mode: TTP, level: 0.1705293, price: 0.17011`,
  then `Сlose deal`. The close order filled at 0.16226.
- §2.3 The bot's next deal opened with a base fill at 0.16279 (:29.483) and
  repeated the pattern on prices 0.17038 (:29.942) and 0.16834 (:31.104).
  `Generate current deal orders @ 0.17043` at :28.914 shows the same lag.
- §2.4 The feed prices trace the fill prices from §2.1 about 12–13 s later and
  in the same order. That is a delayed replay of real ticks, not one cached
  value. It is not a read of a cached last price after the fill either:
  `checkTrailing` is only called from `priceUpdateCallback`, which stores the
  incoming message's own price just before calling it.
- §2.5 Each tick carries the venue's own event time through the feed
  (`time = +trade.eventTime`, from Bybit's `ts`, in milliseconds on every
  connector). `priceUpdateCallback` keys freshness on it and drops only ticks
  older than the last tick. Nothing compares a tick with the fills.

## §3 Assumption

- §3.1 (hypothesis) Arming on a tick whose exchange time precedes the deal's
  latest fill causes §1.2. A harness test fails today: it drives the real
  `checkTrailing` with a fill recorded at T and a tick stamped T − 12 s above
  the post-fill take-profit line, and the trail arms.

## §4 Required behaviour

- §4.1 The engine records, per deal, the exchange `updateTime` of the latest
  fill it processed (`processFilledOrder`).
- §4.2 `checkTrailing` skips a deal for this tick when the tick's time is
  earlier than that deal's latest fill. It does not arm, does not move
  `bestPrice` or the level, and does not cancel orders.
- §4.3 A tick at or after the latest fill, a deal with no recorded fill, and a
  tick with a non-finite time are all processed as before (§1.3).
- §4.4 The record is dropped with the deal's other per-deal check state
  (`removeDealFromStopLossMethods`).

## §5 Out of scope

- §5.1 Why the feed lagged (connector or worker backlog). This guard holds
  whatever the cause.
- §5.2 The trailing trigger path in `checkDealsStopLoss`. It acts only on an
  armed trail. Ticks reaching it are monotonic in exchange time and are never
  older than the tick that armed it, and §4.2 makes that tick no older than
  the latest fill.
