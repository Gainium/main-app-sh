# 049 — A trailing take profit closes below break even

## 1. Definition

### 1.1 Expected

`trailingMode: 'ttp'` is the armed state of a **trailing take profit**: the
deal has already traded through its take-profit price, and the trail exists to
capture *more* than that take profit while giving back at most
`trailingTpPerc`. It is a profit-taking mechanism. On a bot with stop loss
switched off (`useSl: false`) it is the *only* thing that can close the deal.

Therefore: **a close whose trigger is the trailing take-profit arm must never
book a loss.** If the price that would execute the close is at or below the
deal's break-even price — its average entry plus the round-trip taker fee —
the trail is no longer capturing a profit and must not close. The armed state
is stale, and the engine must disarm it (`trailingLevel = 0`,
`trailingMode = undefined`, `bestPrice = 0`) so that `checkTrailing` re-arms
only once price is back above the take-profit price.

### 1.2 Actual

`checkDealsStopLoss` closes the deal as soon as the live price crosses the
registered level, with no reference to the deal's average entry price:

```ts
const levelHit =
  (this.isLong && last <= priceToClose) ||
  (!this.isLong && last >= priceToClose)
```

For the trailing arm, `priceToClose` is `d.deal.trailingLevel`
(`getDealStopLossPrice` §2.2). Nothing checks that `last` is still above break
even. A trailing take profit therefore becomes an **unbounded stop loss** on a
bot that has no stop loss, executing at whatever the price happens to be when
the check next runs.

`checkMinTp` — the engine's existing profitability floor — cannot help: it is
gated on `useMinTP` and on `dealCloseCondition` being `techInd` or `webhook`,
so it never runs for a `tp` close condition (§3.4).

## 2. Evidence

### 2.1 Production deals (read-only, dashboard GraphQL as the reporter)

Two deals on Kraken spot, both `closeTrigger: trailing`, both at a loss:

| deal | avgPrice | trailingLevel | bestPrice | executed | profit.total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| SOL-EUR | 86.8438 | 89.63038 | 89.81 | 85.44 | **−60.285** |
| XRP-EUR | 1.23011 | 1.26384 | 1.26892 | 1.19691 | **−68.451** |

Deal settings on both (verified via `getBotDeals`):
`useSl: false`, `trailingSl: false`, `useTp: true`, `trailingTp: true`,
`tpPerc: '0.800'`, `trailingTpPerc: '0.200'`, `dealCloseCondition: 'tp'`,
`useMultiTp: false`, `useMinTP: false`, `useFixedTPPrices: null`,
`moveSL: false`.

The armed level was **above** the average in both cases (89.63 > 86.84) — the
level was never the problem. The executed price was far **below** it, because
nothing re-tested the level against the deal's economics before closing.

### 2.2 The same bots' other trailing closes are all profitable

On the same two bots, every other `closeTrigger: trailing` deal returned a
profit (+21.28, +19.13, +71.81). The invariant of §1.1 holds for all of them
and is violated by exactly these two — so the defect is a reachable edge of
the trailing path, not the normal behaviour of it.

### 2.3 How the armed level outlived the price (the triggering sequence)

1. The trail triggered correctly **in profit**. The market SELL was rejected by
   the venue (`EGeneral:Temporary lockout`), which writes a CANCELED MARKET row
   through `handleOrderErrors` (`closeDealById`, the `else` after the
   slippage-retry branch). **Nothing disarms the trailing state there.**
2. `triggerStopLoss` had set `d.closeBySl = true`, which `checkDealsStopLoss`
   skips on — and that flag is **in-memory only**. The deal therefore sat armed
   and unchecked until the worker restarted.
3. On each restart, `checkDealsForStopLossMethods` → `setDealForStopLoss` →
   `getDealStopLossPrice` re-registered the stale `trailingLevel`, the first
   tick was already below it, and the close fired again — each time at a worse
   price, and each time below the average.

### 2.4 A safety-order fill makes the level *worse*, not stale

The safety-order path already resets `bestPrice = 0` when the average moves,
but leaves `trailingMode`/`trailingLevel` armed. On the next tick
`checkTrailing` takes `!d.deal.bestPrice` → `bestPrice = last`, sees
`old.price !== d.deal.bestPrice`, and **recomputes `trailingLevel` from the
current price** — arming the trail ~`trailingTpPerc` below spot while the deal
is under water. So the armed level can be stale *or* freshly recomputed
downwards; both converge on the same close decision.

## 3. Assumption (root-cause hypothesis)

The trailing-take-profit arm of `checkDealsStopLoss` decides the close purely
from `last` vs `priceToClose`, with **no floor tying the close to the deal's
economics**. Every mechanism in §2.3/§2.4 that can leave a bad level armed —
venue-rejected close, worker restart, safety-order fill — reaches the deal
through that one decision. A break-even floor evaluated there is therefore
both necessary and sufficient to prevent the reported harm.

Corollary (§2.3 step 1): the terminal rejection branch should additionally
disarm, so the stale state does not survive the failed close at all rather
than being caught later by the floor.

## 4. Scope of the floor — why break even, not the take-profit price

The floor must be **break even (average entry + round-trip taker fee)**, not
the take-profit price.

`checkTrailing` arms `ttp` at the first tick at or above `trailingTpPrice` and
sets `trailingLevel = last * (1 - trailingTpPerc/100)`. That level is, by
construction, up to `trailingTpPerc` **below** the take-profit price from the
moment it arms. A take-profit-price floor would therefore block the ordinary,
by-design, still-profitable close that happens when price retraces
immediately after arming — a behaviour regression. Break even is the strongest
floor that cannot block a profitable close.

## 5. Acceptance

- §5.1 An armed `ttp` deal whose live price is below break even must **not**
  call `triggerStopLoss`, and must be disarmed
  (`trailingLevel = 0`, `trailingMode = undefined`, `bestPrice = 0`) and
  persisted, so a restart cannot re-register the same level.
- §5.2 An armed `ttp` deal whose live price is above break even closes
  normally, with `closeTrigger = trailing` (spec 042 must stay green).
- §5.3 The floor applies only to the trailing **take-profit** arm. An armed
  trailing **stop loss** (`trailingMode: 'tsl'`) and a plain stop loss are
  loss-taking instruments by definition and must keep closing below break
  even.
- §5.4 The break-even price accounts for the round-trip taker fee and for deal
  direction, using the engine's existing fee idiom.
- §5.5 A close rejected terminally by the venue disarms the trailing state.

## 6. Open questions

- §6.1 `d.closeBySl` remaining in-memory (§2.3 step 2) means a rejected close
  is never retried until a restart. Out of scope here: making it persistent or
  self-releasing changes retry behaviour for *all* close rejections and could
  amplify a venue lockout. Left as a separate concern.
- §6.2 No user-visible message is emitted when a close is rejected. Out of
  scope; noted in the plan's Blast Radius as unaddressed.
