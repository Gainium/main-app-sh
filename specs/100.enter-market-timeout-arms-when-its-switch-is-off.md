# 100 — "Enter Market Timeout" arms when its switch is off

A DCA bot's base-order section has an **Enter Market Timeout** switch
(`settings.useLimitTimeout`) and a seconds field (`settings.limitTimeout`).
Both dashboards present it as opt-in: its tooltip reads "Activate a timer for
limit orders. If the limit order has not been filled within that time, a market
order will be executed instead."

The engine does not treat it as opt-in. `enterMarketTimeout` is initialised to
`35000` and `start()` only overrides it when the switch is on, so a bot with the
switch off still arms a 35 s enter-market timer on every LIMIT base order, and a
limit entry the user chose to control becomes a taker market entry.

## §1 Definition

### §1.1 Expected — the base-order rule table

| `useLimitTimeout` | `notUseLimitReposition` | unfilled LIMIT base order |
|---|---|---|
| off | off | repositioned to the market every 10 s, indefinitely; never sent at market |
| off | on  | left untouched |
| on (N s > 0) | off | repositioned until N s, then sent at market |
| on (N s > 0) | on  | not repositioned; sent at market after N s |
| on, N = 0 or blank | any | as the matching "off" row |

A timeout shorter than the reposition interval (N < 10 s) disables
repositioning, as before.

### §1.2 Actual

With the switch off, `enterMarketTimeout` stays `35000`; `checkBaseOrder` arms
`enterMarketTimer` whenever `enterMarketTimeout !== 0 && !notUseLimitReposition`,
and the timer calls `checkBaseOrder(..., forceMarket = true)`. Rows 1 and 5 of
the table behave like row 3 with N = 35.

### §1.3 Paths that read the same field and must NOT change

The switch sits in the base-order section of both dashboards. Two other paths
read `enterMarketTimeout` and keep their current behaviour:

- **§1.3.1 close-by-limit** (`checkTPOrder`, `closeType === closeByLimit`):
  today goes to market 10 s after the limit close whenever the effective
  timeout is non-zero — i.e. for every bot whose switch is off. Unchanged.
- **§1.3.2 the settled-base-entry top-up window** (spec 057,
  `topUpSettledBaseEntry`): `orderLimitRepositionTimeout + <timeout>` = 45 s for
  a switch-off bot. Unchanged — it must not collapse to 10 s, which would bring
  the live settle paths close to the window's edge.

The grid bot's close (`helper.ts` `closeBotByTp`) never reads these settings
and keeps its own `35000` default; it is out of scope.

## §2 Evidence

Production, 2026-09-19: bot with `startOrderType: LIMIT`,
`useLimitTimeout: false`, `limitTimeout: '0'`, `notUseLimitReposition: false`
(settings read back from the dashboard GraphQL). Its deal was created at
10:45:08.949Z and `Deal … enter market price` logged at 10:45:44.562Z — 35.6 s
later — followed by the forced-market settle of the partially filled base
order.

## §3 Assumption (hypothesis)

The only defect is where the timeouts are derived: `start()` (core and the
parent's override) gates the override on the switch and otherwise inherits the
`35000` constructor default. Deriving all three values from the settings in one
place — entry timeout `0` when the switch is off, the legacy value kept for
§1.3 — fixes §1.2 without touching the timer code.

## §4 Required behaviour

- **§4.1** Switch off (or on with 0/blank/unparseable seconds): entry timeout
  is `0`; reposition interval is `10000`.
- **§4.2** Switch on with N s: entry timeout is `N*1000`; reposition interval
  is `0` when `N*1000 < 10000`, else `10000`.
- **§4.3** The close-by-limit / top-up timeout equals what the engine used
  before this change: `N*1000` when the switch is on with a non-blank value,
  else `35000`.
- **§4.4** The values are recomputed from settings on every
  `setClassProperties()` call (idempotent — no sticky `0` reposition interval
  after the user lengthens the timeout).
- **§4.5** For the real `setClassProperties`, a switch-off bot ends with
  `enterMarketTimeout === 0`.
