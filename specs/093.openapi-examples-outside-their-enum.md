# 093 — OpenAPI examples that are not members of their own enum

## §1 Definition

### §1.1 Expected

Every `example:` in `components.schemas` of the published OpenAPI document
(`src/server/v2/openapi-v2.yaml`, served at
`https://api.gainium.io/api/v2/openapi.yaml`) that sits next to an `enum:` is
one of that enum's values, so an integrator who copies it gets an accepted
request.

### §1.2 Actual

Four examples are outside their enum:

| property | example | enum |
|---|---|---|
| `SettingsIndicators.type` | `regular` | indicator list (`RSI`, `ADX`, …) |
| `SettingsIndicators.indicatorInterval` | `oneH` | `1m` … `1w` |
| `DCABotSettings.stopStatus` | `monitoring` | `closed` |
| `DCABotExtended.previousStatus` | `null` | bot statuses |

The first two are rejected by `indicatorCoreConfig` (`shouldBeValidEnumValue`
over `IndicatorEnum` / `ExchangeIntervals` values).

## §2 Evidence

1. A walk over `components.schemas` of the committed YAML and of the live
   document flags exactly the four rows above.
2. `fieldMetadata` in `src/utils/generate-openapi-schemas.ts` is keyed by field
   name only; the `type` entry (written for the bot type, `regular`) is applied
   to `SettingsIndicators.type` too. `indicatorInterval` uses the enum *key*
   `oneH` of `ExchangeIntervals` instead of its value `1h`.
3. `DCABotExtended` is not generated — it is hand-written in the YAML, so a
   generator-side check on generated properties alone would not cover it.
4. Nothing in `main()` compares an example to the enum it resolved.

## §3 Assumption (hypothesis)

The defect is example text only — the enums and validators are right for the
first two rows. A check over the final document before it is written, plus
corrected examples, removes all four and prevents recurrence.

Refinement found while verifying: `DCABotSettings.stopStatus` is typed
`'closed' | 'monitoring'` and its validator accepts every `BotStatusEnum`
value, so `monitoring` was never rejected — its generated enum `["closed"]` is
under-documented (inline literal unions keep only their first member). Out of
scope here (no enum is edited); the example becomes `closed`, which is valid
under both.

## §4 Out of scope

No `enum` and no request validator changes — accepted values are identical.
