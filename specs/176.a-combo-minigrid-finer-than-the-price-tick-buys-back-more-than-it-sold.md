# 176 — A combo minigrid finer than the price tick buys back more than it sold

A combo deal lays a minigrid over each base or safety fill. Its range is
`step %` of the deal's start price, and `createMinigrid` splits that range into
the bot's `gridLevel` (or `baseGridLevels`) levels. `generateBasicGrids` then
rounds every level to the pair's price precision. On a cheap coin with a fine
step, the range can be narrower than `gridLevel` price ticks. Several levels
then round onto the same price.

## §1 Definition

### §1.1 Expected

- **§1.1.1** No two levels of a minigrid `createMinigrid` builds share a
  price. When the requested level count would collapse two of them, the
  minigrid takes the most levels whose rounded prices are all different (at
  least one), and says so in the bot log.
- **§1.1.2** The minigrid trades the way a grid does: a level that fills is
  answered one level away, never at the price that just traded. A short
  minigrid that returns to its top has bought back what it sold, within the
  one lot per level the per-level sizing already allows.
- **§1.1.3** Everything the minigrid derives from its level count — the
  per-level budget split, the sizing, the persisted `settings.levels` that
  every later rebuild reads — uses the reduced count.

### §1.2 Actual

- **§1.2.1** `createMinigrid` passes the requested level count straight to
  `generateBasicGrids`. With a range of 0.0003975 and 10 levels at 4 decimals,
  the 11 level prices round onto 5: `0.0265 ×2, 0.0266 ×2, 0.0267 ×3,
  0.0268 ×2, 0.0269 ×2`.
- **§1.2.2** After a fill, `updateMinigrid` rebuilds the ladder at the fill
  price (`generateGridsOnPrice`): levels above it become SELLs, the rest BUYs,
  and the ONE level nearest the price is left empty. Its twin at the same price
  stays on the ladder as a BUY. After a SELL at 0.0267 the ladder therefore
  rests two BUYs at 0.0267, and the next trade there fills them with no SELL
  above to pay for them.

## §2 Evidence

- **§2.1 Production (read-only), a Bybit USDT perpetual SHORT combo,
  2026-10-18.** The safety level 2 minigrid (range 0.0265025–0.0269, 10
  levels, every fill at 4 decimals) filled twice as many BUYs as SELLs. Its
  fills show the pattern of §1.2.2 directly: a SELL at 0.0266 at 12:12:19
  and a BUY at 0.0266 one second later, then more BUYs there; a SELL at 0.0267
  at 12:20:21 then two BUYs at 0.0267 at 12:20:44; a SELL at 0.0268 at
  12:23:02 then a BUY at 0.0268 at 12:23:39. The surplus plus the base
  minigrid's buys equals the base entry, which is how the exchange position
  reached zero while the deal was open.
- **§2.2 Harness.** `src/bot/combo/subTickLadder.harness.spec.ts` drives the
  real `createMinigrid` → `updateMinigrid` chain on that minigrid. Before the
  fix, after a SELL at 0.0267 the next trade at 0.0267 fills two BUYs there,
  and back at its top the minigrid has bought 863 against 519 sold.

## §3 Assumption

The over-buy comes from levels sharing a rounded price, not from the rebuild
logic itself: with every level on its own price, the level freed by a fill is
the only one at that price. Confirmed by §4 of the harness: the same walk on
the ladder §1.1.1 builds fills nothing at the price it just sold at.

## §4 Not changing

- **§4.1** A ladder whose rounded prices are already all different is built
  exactly as before: same level count, same prices, no log line. A sub-tick
  step alone does not reduce it (4 levels over the §2.1 range round onto 5
  distinct prices and are kept).
- **§4.2** `generateBasicGrids` and `generateGridsOnPrice` are unchanged; the
  grid bot shares them.
- **§4.3** Minigrids that already exist keep their persisted level count. The
  reduction applies to minigrids created after the change.
- **§4.4** The base-order and safety-order sizing that pre-builds a ladder
  (`getBaseOrder`, `createInitialDealOrders`) is unchanged. It sizes the order
  from the budget, which does not depend on how many levels share it.

## Invariants

None added. The state this leaves (a minigrid whose fills do not balance) is
already the subject of spec 175's flat-deal handling, and a level count is
not observable from production data without the pair's precision at creation
time.
