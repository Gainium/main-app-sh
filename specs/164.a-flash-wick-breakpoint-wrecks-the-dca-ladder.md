# 164 — A flash-wick breakpoint wrecks the DCA ladder

Bug spec (Definition / Evidence / Assumption).

## §1 Definition

A *grid breakpoint* `{price P, displacedPrice D}` on a DCA deal moves ladder
level `P` to `D`. It exists for one situation: the market moved past level `P`
before that level was resting, so the level is placed at the market price
instead (`createCurrentDealOrders`), or a level filled at a price other than
its own (`updateDeal`).

Expected:

- §1.1 A level whose order has **already filled** is not a current order. It
  is not re-sent, and no breakpoint is recorded for it (the market did not
  skip it). This holds even when a burst of fills is still being processed
  and the deal's in-memory filled count lags. A partly filled level is still
  resting and stays a current order.
- §1.2 A market price **beyond the deal's deepest ladder level** is not a
  place to move one level to. No breakpoint is recorded; the level keeps its
  own price, as it would have had the market never moved.
- §1.3 When the ladder is built, a stored breakpoint whose `displacedPrice`
  is beyond the ladder's deepest configured level (or is not a positive
  finite number) is ignored. This also covers breakpoints already stored by
  §1.1/§1.2 before this fix. Raising the level count on such a deal places
  the real next levels.
- §1.4 A breakpoint inside the ladder's range is applied exactly as before
  (a real gap still moves the level to the market price).

Actual: a one-second flash wick to ~1% of the price, during a burst of
safety-order fills, recorded breakpoints that moved two levels to the wick
price. Every later rebuild (fill, settings edit, reload) moved those levels
there. The "not behind the previous level" guard then pushed each later level
one tick further, down to zero, and the ladder stopped. A 35-level ladder
became 19 levels ending at the wick price, and raising the count placed only
two orders, both near zero.

## §2 Evidence

- Prod deal (paper Kraken, 0G-USD, 1% × 30 → 35 levels, start 0.315):
  `gridBreakpoints = [{0.258→0.002},{0.255→0.002}]`. Open safety orders are at
  0.002 and 0.001. Levels 0.258 and 0.255 both FILLED at their own prices.
- Kraken's own 15-minute candle for that pair has a low of 0.002 in the
  minute of the burst. The wick was a real print; `getLatestPrice` returned it.
- Bot log of the burst: five regular fills (0.271…0.258) processed
  interleaved. The 0.261 fill was processed before the 0.271/0.268/0.265
  fills, so `left = ordersCount − filled` was three too high and the slice
  of current orders began at the already-FILLED 0.258 level. The existing
  check only looks for a *resting* order at that level, did not find one,
  saw the market below it and wrote `{0.258 → latestPrice}`. The next
  rebuild (stale ladder) did the same for 0.255.
- The real `createInitialDealOrders` given the stored breakpoints returns
  `… 0.265 0.261 0.002 0.001` (19 levels). The same call without them
  returns 35 levels down to 0.205.

## §3 Assumption (hypothesis)

The breakpoint path trusts two things it cannot know: that a missing resting
order means the level was skipped (false for a level that filled), and that
the market price is a sane place for a level (false outside the deal's
coverage). The ladder build then applies whatever is stored with no bound. Fix
both creation checks (§1.1, §1.2) and bound the build (§1.3), keeping §1.4.

## §4 Out of scope

- The `left` lag itself during interleaved fill processing. §1.1 removes its
  consequence on the checked rebuild (`noCheck = false`: fills, settings
  edits). The unchecked callers keep today's slice.
- The §1.3 bound for custom, indicator and ATR/ADR ladders, whose deepest
  level has no closed form. §1.2 covers their creation side, and a breakpoint
  that is not a positive finite number is ignored for every ladder type.
