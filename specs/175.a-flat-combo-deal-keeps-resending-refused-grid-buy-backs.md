# 175 — A flat combo deal keeps re-sending grid orders the exchange refuses as un-reducible

A futures combo deal is open, and the exchange holds no position for it. Each
of its minigrids still has closing-side levels on its ladder. For a short those
are reduce-only BUYs; for a long, reduce-only SELLs. The exchange refuses every
one of them with a "no position to reduce" reason. Bybit words it as
`current position is zero, cannot fix reduce-only order qty`. The refusal
leaves the ladder untouched. So every later fill, smart-grid re-placement or
orders check sends the same levels again, and the deal stays `open` for as long
as the bot runs.

The deal never closes on its own. A combo deal closes on the `base` trigger
only when the base minigrid runs out of closing levels. Here the base minigrid
still holds them, because its ladder is rebuilt from the price, not from what
the deal's fills actually hold.

This spec handles the open deal. Spec 173 and the orphaned-ladder cleanup handle
the ladders of deals that are already closed.

## §1 Definition

### §1.1 Expected

- **§1.1.1** The exchange refuses a closing-side combo grid order (`dealGrid`,
  futures, reduce-only side) with a reason in `positionAlreadyClosedReasons`.
  The bot then asks the exchange whether it still holds a position for the
  deal. It uses the same probe, cooldown and two-agreeing-readings rule as spec
  005 (`reconcileDealPosition`, `positionProbeBackoff`).
- **§1.1.2** Two readings agree that no position is held. The bot then closes
  the deal through the combo base-minigrid close:
  `closeDealById(closeByMarket, …, DCACloseTriggerEnum.base)`. That path
  cancels the deal's resting orders. Its market close is then refused as
  position-already-closed, and the deal is booked closed with no order. The
  user gets the spec-005 "no open position" deal event. The deal sends no more
  grid orders.
- **§1.1.3** The exchange reports a position on the deal's side, or the probe
  gets no answer. The deal is left open and nothing about its grid trading
  changes. An answered probe clears the probe cooldown, as in spec 005.
- **§1.1.4** Only one probe per deal runs at a time. A burst of refusals for one
  deal therefore cannot supply both readings at the same moment.
- **§1.1.5** None of this runs for opening-side grid orders, for safety or
  base orders, for spot bots, for any other refusal reason, or for a deal that
  is not `open` or is already closing.

### §1.2 Actual

- **§1.2.1** The refusal goes only to `handleOrderErrors`. That method reports it
  under the `Futures position` subtype, which is hidden from the user. Nothing
  asks the exchange about the position. The deal stays open, and its ladders
  re-send the refused levels for hours.

## §2 Evidence

Production, a Bybit linear SHORT combo, 2026-10-18 (read-only):

- A deal opened with a market SELL base. Three hidden safety levels got their
  virtual fills and their own minigrids. Over three hours, its FILLED grid
  BUYs added up to exactly its base plus its FILLED grid SELLs. By its own
  orders, the deal's net position was zero, and it was still `open` with four
  ladders.
- In that time, the same few reduce-only BUY levels were sent to the exchange
  and refused with `current position is zero, cannot fix reduce-only order
  qty` on every grid event. The refused level is sent again with a new client
  order id each time.
- On the same bot, the `closeDealById` → "rejected because the position is
  already closed … Closing deal without order" path ran many times on other
  deals that day. That is the close §1.1.2 reuses, and it works on this venue.

### §2.1 Where the extra buy-back came from

The deal's own FILLED orders, grouped by minigrid:

| ladder | sold | bought back |
|---|---|---|
| base (market entry) | entry only | ≈ 20% of the entry |
| safety level 2 (hidden, virtual fill) | 6 grid sells | 12 grid buys, ≈ 1.9× what it sold |
| safety level 3 (hidden, virtual fill) | 7 grid sells | 7 grid buys, the same amount |
| safety level 4 (hidden, virtual fill) | none | none |

The level-2 ladder's surplus plus the base ladder's buys equals the entry. So
the cause is neither of the two triage hypotheses:

- **Not the virtual seeding.** A hidden level's minigrid starts at the deal's
  opening price, below its own range. Every level is then a SELL, which opens
  short, and it holds no position until those sells fill.
- **Not two ladders counting the same fills.** Every buy belongs to one
  minigrid, and the base and level-3 ladders each balance.

It is ONE ladder that bought back more than it sold. That ladder's range spans
four price ticks, but it has ten levels. Several levels round to the same price,
and it bought at one price five times after a single sell at that price. This
is a separate defect in how minigrid levels are sized against the price tick,
and it is out of scope here (§3). This spec makes the deal stop and close
whatever produced the overshoot.

## §3 Assumption / out of scope

- The fix acts only on what the exchange says about the position. It does not
  change the virtual fills of hidden safety levels at deal open. It also does
  not change how minigrid ladders are generated, rebuilt or traded while a real
  position exists.
- The ladder over-buy in §2.1 (levels finer than the tick collapsing onto one
  price) is tracked separately.
- Capping a deal's combined buy-back at what it holds would change how normal
  grid trading sizes its orders. It is not done here.

## Invariants

None added. The defect is not silent: every refusal is logged, and the deal
settles itself under §1.1.2.
