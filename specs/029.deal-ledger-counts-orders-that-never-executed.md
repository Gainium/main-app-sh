# 029 — the deal balance ledger counts orders that never executed

A combo bot reported deal profits of 7–11% on a bot configured to take 3%, and
the exchange balance did not move by anything like the reported amount. The
report guessed at fees or grid spacing; neither is involved.

Related: spec `024` rebuilds the ledger when it has been LOST. This spec is
about the rebuild itself producing a wrong ledger from intact inputs.

## 1. Definition

A deal's `currentBalances` is a running ledger of what the deal has actually
bought and sold. `dcaHelper.updateDealBalances` re-derives it from the deal's
own order rows whenever a bot loads or a deal is recalculated:

```
currentBalances = initialBalances + Σ over the deal's orders of
  ( executedQty * (side === buy ? +1 : -1)          on base
  , executedQty * price * (side === buy ? -1 : +1)  on quote )
```

`closeDeal` then books
`profit.total = currentBalances.quote - initialBalances.quote +
(currentBalances.base - initialBalances.base) * lastPrice - commDeal`.

### 1.1 Expected

Only quantity a venue actually executed may enter that sum. A resting order
that was cancelled without trading, and an order that never reached a venue at
all, contribute nothing — they moved no funds, so they must not move the
ledger, and the residual base position `closeDeal` marks at `lastPrice` must be
base the deal really holds.

### 1.2 Actual

`updateDealBalances` counts two kinds of row that executed nothing:

**(a) A cancelled order that never reached a venue.** Its filter admits any
row with `status: 'FILLED'` **or** `executedQty !== 0`, so a CANCELED row with
a non-zero `executedQty` is read as a partial fill. Combo safety orders are
persisted the moment the deal opens, before any venue call, carrying
`orderId: '-1'` (the placeholder `main.ts` uses for "never placed"),
`fills: []`, and `executedQty` equal to `origQty`. Cancelling one leaves a row
that is indistinguishable, to this filter, from a fully filled order.

**(b) A row that reports zero executed quantity.** The quantity expression is

```
(v.executedQty && !isNaN(+v.executedQty) && isFinite(+v.executedQty) && +v.executedQty)
  ? +v.executedQty
  : v.typeOrder === dealStart ? +v.executedQty : +v.origQty
```

`+'0'` is falsy, so a row that explicitly says it executed nothing falls
through to `origQty` — the quantity it was *planned* for. A `FILLED` row with
`executedQty: '0'` and `price: '0'` (the shape spec `028` documents) therefore
adds its whole planned size to the base leg, at price zero, so the ledger gains
base that cost nothing.

Both inflate the base leg. `closeDeal` marks that inflation at `lastPrice` and
books it as realised profit, on the deal, on the bot aggregate
(`this.data.profit`) and in the user's profit history via `saveProfitToDb` —
none of which is ever recomputed. The same inflated ledger is what sizes the
closing take-profit, so the deal also tries to sell base it does not hold and
the venue rejects the order.

## 2. Evidence

Read-only from production on 2026-09-09: the dashboard GraphQL
(`getComboBotDeals`, `getBotOrders`), the read-only prod `mongo` channel, and
the read-only prod `logs` helper. Combo bot `6a97511e110ce258287e4151`,
VVV-USDC on coinbase, 35 closed deals.

### 2.1 The bot's reported profit is 2.9× what its own fills support

Replaying every deal's own executed fills against its `initialBalances` and
marking the residual at each deal's `lastPrice`:

| | `profit.total`, summed over the 35 closed deals |
|---|---|
| reported | **381.25** |
| supported by the deals' own executed fills | **130.20** |

The 251.04 difference sits in **four** deals, which between them report 297.70:

| deal | `currentBalances.base` | base its fills support | reported | supported |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `6aa0060c…` | 26.280 | −0.282 | 95.13 | 0.74 |
| `6aa02ef5…` | 26.796 | −0.075 | 74.04 | 18.75 |
| `6aa06930…` | 26.255 | −0.042 | 71.51 | 8.26 |
| `6aa107ec…` | 22.110 | −1.233 | 57.02 | 1.10 |

The other 31 deals agree within 3.6 and mostly within 0.5.

### 2.2 The ledger reproduces bit-for-bit from the two miscounts

Deal `6aa0060c…` has 61 order rows. Its genuinely executed fills (one base
order and 21 grid legs, every one carrying a venue order id) net **−0.145**
base and **+4.4255** quote. Adding what §1.2 wrongly counts:

| source | base | quote |
|---|---|---|
| genuinely executed fills | −0.145 | +4.4255 |
| (a) 3 CANCELED safety rows, `orderId: '-1'`, `executedQty === origQty` | +24.923 | −450.0051 |
| (b) 1 `FILLED` row, `executedQty: '0'`, `price: '0'`, counted at `origQty` | +1.639 | 0 |
| **total** | **+26.417** | **−445.5796** |

`initialBalances.quote` is 900.2923896, so the ledger comes out
`{base: 26.417, quote: 454.7128275}`. `closeDeal` adds the closing take-profit
(0.137 @ 20.4954) and books

```
457.5206973 − 900.2923896 + 26.28 × 20.4954 − 0.7180994272 = 95.12932027278998
```

which is the value stored on the deal, to every digit. From the corrected
ledger the same arithmetic books **0.7356**.

### 2.3 The venue itself says the base was not there

The deal's take-profit was first placed for 26.392 — the inflated position —
and rejected for insufficient balance. Adaptive close re-placed it at the
account's actual free balance, and the deal records the pair on the closing
order: `ac: { before: 26.392, after: 0.137 }`. The same `before ≫ after`
signature is on all four bad deals (26.661/0.077, 26.214/0.044, 23.337/1.238)
and on no healthy one. The prod log for the deal shows the matching sizing
line, `base order qty 31.221 taken from the position … (size 31.22, counted
fills 0)`.

Prices confirm it independently: the three phantom safety orders rest at
18.6447, 18.0681 and 17.4915, and the pair never traded below 18.7515 while
the deal was open. They could not have filled.

### 2.4 The two miscounted shapes are separable fleet-wide

Every CANCELED order row in production carrying a non-zero `executedQty`:

| | rows |
|---|---|
| `orderId === '-1'` (never reached a venue), `executedQty === origQty` | 14,721 |
| carries a venue order id — genuine partial fill | 360 |
| carries a venue order id — genuine full fill | 32 |

The split is total: no row without a venue id is a genuine fill, and no genuine
fill lacks a venue id. Shape (b) is rarer — a few dozen `FILLED` deal rows with
a zero `executedQty`, plus liquidation rows that this ledger never reads.

## 3. Assumption

*Hypothesis, not fact.* The wrong figure is produced entirely by
`updateDealBalances` counting the two shapes in §1.2. If that is right, feeding
the deal's real order rows through the real `updateDealBalances` reproduces
`{26.417, 454.7128275}` today and `{−0.145, 904.7179}` once each shape
contributes zero, and driving the real `closeDeal` over those two ledgers books
95.12932027278998 and ~0.7356 respectively.

If instead the fills themselves are misrecorded, or `initialBalances` is wrong,
correcting the two shapes will not land on the figures in §2.2 and the
assumption is refuted.

## 4. Required behaviour

- **§4.1** An order that never reached a venue contributes nothing to the
  ledger. `orderId === '-1'` on a terminal, non-filled row is that statement:
  there is no venue-side order, so there is no execution.
- **§4.2** A row that reports a readable `executedQty` contributes exactly
  that, including when it is zero. The planned `origQty` is a fallback only
  when `executedQty` cannot be read at all, and never for the base order, whose
  partial fills the deal must never guess at.
- **§4.3** A genuine partial or full fill on a cancelled order still counts —
  the 392 rows in §2.4 that carry a venue order id must be unaffected.
- **§4.4** A healthy deal's ledger and booked profit do not move by a single
  unit.
- **§4.5** `closeDeal`'s spec `024` lost-ledger rebuild replays the same order
  rows and must apply §4.1/§4.2 too, or it repairs a lost ledger into a
  phantom one.

## 5. Out of scope

- Why the safety-order rows are persisted with `executedQty === origQty` before
  they are placed. Read-side correctness is what decides the money here, and
  14,721 such rows already exist and cannot be un-written.
- Restacking the four already-closed deals and the bot's aggregate totals. The
  profit figures are persisted and no code recomputes them; that is a migration,
  not a code change, and is left open.
- The deal-close path running more than once on one deal (open finding F221).
  It re-enters the profit accrual block, which is a separate defect from what
  the accrual block computes.
