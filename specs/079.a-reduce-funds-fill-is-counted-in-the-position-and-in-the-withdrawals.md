# 079 — A reduce-funds fill is counted in the position AND in the withdrawals

Bug spec. Found while investigating a run of start refusals (spec 078).

## 1. Definition

### 1.1 Expected

A deal's gross entry volume is reconstructed as `|deal.size| + executed
reduce-funds` (`dca/baseOrderQty.ts` `grossEntryVolume`). That holds on one
premise: `deal.size` is the live position and is therefore NET of every
executed withdrawal, so the withdrawals have to be added back.

### 1.2 Actual

The two halves are written at different times. `deal.reduceFunds` is appended
the instant the reduce order's fill is handled; `deal.size` is refreshed from
the deal's usage a moment later, on the `saveDeal().then()` that follows. In
between, the withdrawal is in BOTH terms and the reconstructed entry volume is
high by exactly the amount withdrawn.

## 2. Evidence

A deal that had entered 60.3 (base order only; no safety order ever filled)
withdrew 25.63:

    <fill>  Reduce TP order FILLED <id>
    <+1ms>  Deal <id> holds more than its order rows account for — base order
            qty 85.93 taken from the position, not the 60.3 on record
            (size 60.3, counted fills 0)

`85.93 − 60.30 = 25.63`, the withdrawal to the cent. The line never repeats
after the next reload, which is the refresh landing and the premise becoming
true again — a transient window, not a corrupt deal.

A second deal, on another pair and another day, shows the identical signature
(`counted fills 0`, recorded size equal to the base-order row, difference
equal to the reduce fill exactly).

Fleet-wide over two weeks the warning itself is common — thousands of lines,
hundreds of deals — but essentially all of it is the branch's INTENDED case:
order rows missing from the map after a worker restart. Only the two above
carry the reduce-funds signature. Rare, and worth fixing anyway, because of
what is sized in that window.

## 3. Consequence

A take-profit sized inside the window is built from `filledQty + baseOrderQty`,
and `resolveBaseOrderQty` returns the inflated figure (`source: 'position'`).
For the deal above that is a close for 60.3 against 34.67 actually held — 74%
over. On a one-way account, a fill of that order does not close the position,
it flips it: 25.63 the other way.

Neither observed deal realised it (no take-profit was placed on either in that
window), so this is a latent over-size, not a recorded loss.

## 4. Solution

`src/bot/dca/reduceFundsFill.ts` — `bookReduceFundsFill` appends the entry to
`reduceFunds` and takes the same quantity out of `size` as one step, so
`|size| + reduceFunds` is unchanged by the booking and the premise holds at
every instant. The sign of `size` is preserved and its magnitude never goes
past zero: a withdrawal larger than the recorded position means the position
was already stale, and a negative magnitude would turn a short into a long.

The later `updateUsage` recomputes `size` from usage and ASSIGNS it, so this is
not a decrement that can be applied twice. The reduce branch's `saveDeal` now
persists `size` alongside `reduceFunds` for the same reason: the two fields
are one fact and must not be persisted apart.

## 5. Tests

`src/bot/dca/reduceFundsFill.spec.ts` — the booking moves the quantity between
the two fields, leaves the gross entry volume unchanged, keeps a short's sign,
clamps at zero, and records a fill whose quantity cannot be read without
touching `size`. The last test drives `resolveBaseOrderQty` with the live
numbers and pins both readings: the stale window returns 85.93
(`source: 'position'`), the booked state returns 60.3 (`source: 'order'`).

## 6. Open, not addressed here

`dca/tpCoverageReconcile.ts` `trackedPosition` SUBTRACTS the executed
reduce-funds from `|deal.size|`, i.e. it reads `deal.size` as gross of
withdrawals — the opposite convention to `grossEntryVolume`, which adds them
back. Both cannot be right about the same field. If `deal.size` settles net,
as the datum in `baseOrderQty.ts` and this spec's own evidence both say, then
the coverage reconciler under-states the position on every settled deal that
used reduce funds. That is a reporting path, not an order-sizing one, and it
needs its own evidence before anything is changed.
