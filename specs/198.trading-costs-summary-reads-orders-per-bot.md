# 198 — The trading-costs summary reads every bot's orders one bot at a time

Bug spec (Definition / Evidence / Assumption). Area: `tradingCostsSummary`
and the bot field `tradingCosts` (main-app spec 050 §5).

## §1 Definition

- **§1.1 Expected.** An account's (or a bot's) closed-deal trading costs load
  within seconds however many bots the account has. Historical deals (closed
  before close-time facts were recorded) and deals on a fee-token venue still
  need their bot's orders, but the number of `orders` reads grows with the
  number of _chunks_ of bots, not with the number of bots.
- **§1.2 Actual.** `runAggregate` walks the deals and, for each deal of a bot
  not cached yet, awaits `historicalFacts(botId)` (one MARKET-order read, one
  bot-settings read, one "fee not reported" probe) and `botUnbooked(botId)`
  (one fee-bearing-order read) before moving to the next deal. The reads are
  strictly serial: an account with B such bots costs about 4·B round trips
  before the query answers, past the dashboard's 30 s ceiling once B is in the
  low thousands. The query fails and the fees tooltip shows nothing.
- **§1.3 Unchanged.** Every figure the query returns — fees, slippage, gross,
  net, the estimated/reconstructed flags, the off-pair fee amount and its
  partial flag — is identical for the same data. Off-pair fees are still
  valued in one price batch per bot, bot after bot (the per-call candle-day
  cap applies per bot, and the price cache sees the same reads in the same
  order, as before).

## §2 Evidence

- Slow-query log: `tradingCostsSummary` calls far past the dashboard's
  ceiling, on accounts with thousands of bots and tens of thousands of
  historical deals; durations ragged (accumulated work, no pinned timeout
  band).
- Local reproduction on a synthetic account (1,500 bots × 6 historical deals
  on a fee-token venue): 6,002 reads (4,500 `orders`, 1,500 `dcaBots`), 31 s
  at local latency, 165 s at 20 ms per read.
- **Second round (the chunked order reads alone did not fix it).** With them
  in place a cold call was still past the ceiling while the chunked reads
  themselves took seconds. The rest is the off-pair fee valuation: valued bot
  after bot, each bot's missing candle days are read before the next bot
  starts — usually one or two days per bot, so the 4-read concurrency bound
  almost never applies — and when an account's fee days add up to more than
  the minute-price cache holds, it is cleared mid-run and days are read again.
  A replay of a real account's per-bot fee days through `FeeAssetPrices` with
  a fake source: 462 strictly serial reads (191 distinct days); 122 s at
  500 ms per read, 27 s at 100 ms. A day the archive has not stored yet is
  backfilled from the venue on read, which makes each serial read slower.

## §3 Assumption (hypothesis), and what the tests check

- **§3.1** The cost is the per-bot serial awaits in the deal loop, not the
  fee-asset price lookups (those are already per pair and UTC day).
  Test: an account whose deals span more than one `IN_CHUNK` of bots reads
  `orders` a constant number of times per chunk.
- **§3.2** Reading the same filters with `botId: {$in: chunk}` and grouping by
  `botId` gives each bot exactly what its own read gave. Test: per-bot
  slippage, estimated flag and off-pair fees are attributed to the right bot
  when several bots share one read.
- **§3.3** The remaining cost is waiting on each bot's candle days in turn.
  Reading the days a chunk of bots needs ahead of valuing them, several at a
  time, and answering the bot-by-bot valuation from those reads, removes the
  wait without changing any value. Tests: `warm` reads each day once with more
  than one read in flight, and `prices` afterwards gives what a cold `prices`
  gives without reading; an account of many bots on a fee-token venue values
  their off-pair fees with more than one candle read in flight and the same
  totals.
