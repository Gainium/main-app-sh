# 047 — A standing dust refusal is still silent, and a fully-exited deal never settles

Issue #763. Amends spec `043` §4.3 (#755) and extends spec
`005.zombie-deal-venue-position-reconciliation` (#630) to spot.

## §1 Definition

`getTPOrder` refuses to build a close that would sit under the venue's minimum
notional. Spec `043` §4.3 required a refusal that keeps holding to be reported
to the user, and §4.1 rewrote the quantity that refusal measures and prints.

### §1.1 Expected

- A refusal carrying a real `dealId` **reaches the user**, because nothing the
  bot does will clear it and only the user can act on it.
- The quantity the refusal names is the quantity the deal still holds. It is
  never negative — a deal cannot hold less than nothing.
- A deal whose position is provably gone is **settled**, not refused forever.
  Spot has the same zombie shape `005` fixed for futures: the exit filled, the
  deal stayed `open`, and every worker start re-asks for a close that can never
  be built.

### §1.2 Actual

- The report never fires. `043` §4.3 gates it on the 5th consecutive refusal,
  counted in an in-memory per-process `Map`, on the assumption that the refusal
  "recurs roughly once a minute per deal". It does not: it recurs **once per
  worker start**. No deal reaches 5 within one process lifetime, so every
  refusal is written to the hidden lane and the user is told nothing.
- The refusal prints a **negative** quantity when the deal's filled closes
  exceed its recorded size.
- A spot deal whose base left the account stays `open` indefinitely, re-refusing
  a close on every worker start.

## §2 Evidence

### §2.1 The report never fires

Production `botmessages`, all `Close order is below the exchange minimum` rows
carrying a real deal id, over the 5 days since `043` shipped:

| window | rows | occurrences | `showUser: true` |
|---|---|---|---|
| since the `043` deploy landed | 22 | 25 | **0** |
| whole 5-day window | 101 | 222 | **0** |

The most any single deal accumulated in the post-deploy window is **4** — the
gate needs 5. The three recurring deals each logged exactly 4, and their
timestamps cluster within ~20 s of each other and of every other bot's
occurrence (`08:16:22`, `03:44:19`, `09:47:21`, `04:49:40`), which is the
signature of a worker start sweeping its deals, not of a per-minute tick.

The engine log for one such deal over the same 5 days holds **13 lines**, spread
hours apart, one per worker start.

`043`'s own §2.2 measurement was taken *before* §4.1 landed, when the block
still refused every long close unconditionally — that is where the "once a
minute" figure came from. §4.1 removed the two closeable shapes from the
population, and what is left recurs on the deal-load path only.

### §2.2 The negative quantity

The same deal's log lines, either side of the `043` deploy:

```
2026-09-12T08:51:22  ... Deal … qty 1   at price 0.0912 is under 5 USDT
2026-09-13T08:16:22  ... Deal … qty -68 at price 0.0912 is under 5 USDT
```

`043` §4.1 replaced the printed `tpOrder.qty` with `heldQty`, which is
`trackedPosition` — `|deal.size|` less `tpHistory`, filled closes and reduce
funds. A deal recorded with `size: 0` and one FILLED close of 68 evaluates to
`-68`. The comparison that uses it is correct (`qtyForFloor > heldQty` refuses
either way); only the printed value is wrong, and it is the part the user reads.

### §2.3 The deal that never settles

A binance spot deal opened 2025-10-11 was still `status: 'open'` 11 months
later with `size: 0, value: 0, cost: 0`, an empty `tpHistory` and exactly two
orders, both FILLED on the day it opened:

| type | side | qty | price |
|---|---|---|---|
| `dealStart` | BUY | 68 | 0.0894 |
| `dealTP` | SELL | 68 | 0.0935 |

The position was fully exited the day it opened and the deal was never closed.
`reconcileDealPosition` — the check `005` added for exactly this — returns at
its first line for spot (`if (!this.futures || !this.exchange) return false`),
and nothing else in the engine asks whether there is anything left to close.

## §3 Assumption

Three distinct causes, one surface:

1. `043` §4.3 sized its report gate against a recurrence rate that its own §4.1
   fix removed, and put the counter in per-process memory. A gate of 5 over a
   condition that fires once per process start is unreachable by construction.
   Rate-limiting the *raise* is already `processError`'s job
   (`errorRaiseBackoff`, per bot and subtype, Redis-backed and therefore
   restart-proof); the second, weaker gate in `getTPOrder` only suppressed it.
2. `043` §4.1 printed a signed ledger residual as a holding.
3. `005`'s reconciliation was written against `futures_getPositions` and so
   could not be asked on spot, leaving the spot half of the zombie shape
   unhandled.

## §4 Requirements

### §4.1 Report a refusal carrying a deal id on its first occurrence

Drop the consecutive-refusal counter. A refusal with a real `dealId` reports
through `handleErrors` with `sendError`/`setEvent` immediately; `processError`'s
existing per-(bot, subType) `errorRaiseBackoff` is the rate limit, and it is the
only one that survives a restart.

`setError` stays false, exactly as `043` §4.3 required: one unclosable dust
position must not put an otherwise healthy multi-deal bot into an error state.

`043` §4.4 is untouched — a refusal with no `dealId` is still a debug line and
writes nothing.

### §4.2 Never print a negative holding

Clamp the quantity named in the refusal at zero. Display only: the comparison
that decides the refusal keeps the signed value.

### §4.3 Reconcile a spot deal against the account balance

Extend `reconcileDealPosition` to long spot deals. Where futures asks
`futures_getPositions`, spot asks the account balance and reads the base asset.
The verdict is a pure function, sibling to `reconcileDealAgainstVenue`:

- **provably gone** — the account holds *less base than the pair's minimum order
  quantity*. There is no close order that could be built for this deal from this
  account, whoever the remaining units belong to.
- **still there** — the account holds at least that much. A spot balance is not
  per-deal, so this is deliberately one-directional: it can only ever veto.
- **unknown** — the venue did not answer. Never settles a deal, for the reason
  `005` gives: booking a deal closed while the position is in fact open abandons
  it with no take profit and no stop loss.

Short spot deals are out of scope — their position is not base, and the
equivalent reading is a different question.

`005`'s two-agreeing-probes rule, its probe cooldown and its `clear()` on a
contradicting reading all apply unchanged.

### §4.4 Ask it where the deal is actually stuck

A refusal that carries a `dealId` **and** a held quantity at or below zero is
the engine saying its own ledger has nothing left to close. That is the point at
which §4.3's reconciliation is asked. When it settles the deal, the refusal is
not reported — the condition is over, not standing.

### §4.5 Nothing else moves

A deal whose close clears the notional floor, a refusal with no `dealId`, a
refusal with a positive holding, and every futures reconciliation path must come
back byte-identical.
