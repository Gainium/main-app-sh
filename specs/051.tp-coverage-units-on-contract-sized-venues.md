# 051 — take-profit coverage is measured in two units on contract-sized venues

Issue #788. Follow-up to `013` (#696), `014` (#700) and `017` (#702), all on
the same `checkTpCoverage` / `reconcileTpCoverage` path.

The issue was reported against COIN-M, and COIN-M is §1–§5 below. Tracing the
root cause to `getOrder` showed it is not COIN-M-specific: the asymmetry is
that `getOrder` converts ONE of the two quantities back to our unit, and it
reaches this check on **every venue the engine sizes in contracts** — two
families, not one. The second is OKX and KuCoin futures (`sizedInContracts`),
§4.6, and it carries nine times the measured exposure of the family the issue
was filed about.

## 1. Definition

### 1.1 Expected

`checkTpCoverage` answers one question: does the take-profit resting on the
venue still cover the position the deal holds? Both sides of that comparison
must be stated in the SAME unit, and on an inverse (COIN-M) deal that unit is
**contracts** — the unit the venue trades in, the unit `sendOrderToExchange`
converts into on the way out (`main.ts:7188-7196`), and the unit
`calculateAbstractPosition` keeps the position in (`main.ts:5591-5597`).

A COIN-M deal whose resting take-profit sells exactly the contracts the deal
holds must read `covered`, and a COIN-M deal that is genuinely uncovered must
read `under`.

### 1.2 Actual

The probe is built from the venue's own figures, which on COIN-M are
**contracts**, and compared against `trackedPosition`, which returns **base**
derived from `deal.size`. The two are off by a factor of `price / contractSize`
— roughly 772× on a `$100` BTC contract — so every COIN-M deal reads `over` by
almost the whole resting quantity, and the mirror case (a genuinely uncovered
COIN-M position) would read `covered` because a real shortfall is far smaller
than that factor.

### 1.3 Not the same defect as `014`

`014` was a ~0.1% fee band. This is a unit error three orders of magnitude
wide; the fee tolerance cannot and must not absorb it.

### 2.3 Where the units diverge, traced

* `sendOrderToExchange` (`main.ts:7188-7196`) sends
  `round(origQty × price / quoteAsset.minAmount, 0)` when
  `this.coinm && !this.isBitget` — so the venue holds **contracts**.
* `getOrder` (`main.ts:4844`) runs the answer through
  `convertOrderExecutedQty` (`main.ts:6988-7005`), which converts
  `executedQty` back to **base** — and leaves `origQty` alone.
* `mergeCommonOrderWithOrder` (`main.ts:5415`) spreads `{ ...co }` and
  overrides `executedQty` but not `origQty`, so the merged order that reaches
  `venueConfirmed` carries `origQty` in contracts and `executedQty` in base.
* `restingTpQty` (`tpCoverageReconcile.ts:93`) then computes
  `origQty - executedQty` — contracts minus base.
* `trackedPosition` (`tpCoverageReconcile.ts:107`) returns base, from
  `deal.size`.

So the reported figure is not merely "the other unit": it is a mixed-unit
subtraction compared against a third quantity.

## 3. Assumption (hypothesis)

The root cause is that `getOrder` converts **one** of the two quantities back
to our unit and not the other — `result.data.executedQty =
convertOrderExecutedQty(result.data)` (`main.ts:4844`) leaves `origQty` as the
venue stated it — and the probe mapping (`dcaHelper.ts:10706-10713`) then
copies both verbatim into a comparison against `trackedPosition`, which is
ours. `reconcileTpCoverage` itself is unit-agnostic arithmetic and is not at
fault.

Stated that way the scope follows: `convertOrderExecutedQty` has **two**
contract branches (`main.ts:6994-7005`), `coinm && !isBitget` and
`sizedInContracts`, and the defect is one per branch. §4.1 answers the first,
§4.6 the second.

Corollary the fix must respect: **base is not a conserved unit on an inverse
contract.** The same 1158 contracts are `1.3944` BTC at the take-profit price
and `1.5007` BTC at the average entry price. Converting the venue's contracts
to base at the ORDER's price and comparing against `deal.size` therefore
reports a healthy deal as `under` by the whole take-profit distance (here
`1.5012 - 1.3944 = 0.1068` BTC, 7.1%) — a smaller version of the same defect,
in the direction that triggers the `017` re-arm branch. The comparison has to
happen in contracts.

## 4. Requirements

### 4.1 The comparison happens in contracts on COIN-M

When `this.coinm && !this.isBitget` — the same predicate `sendOrderToExchange`
and `convertOrderExecutedQty` key off, so the check cannot disagree with the
code that placed the order — both sides are expressed in contracts:

* resting: `origQty` is already contracts; `executedQty` is base and is
  converted with the order's own price, `qty × price / contractSize`.
* tracked: `trackedPosition` is unit-agnostic arithmetic — `size` less
  everything already sold — so it is handed contract-denominated TERMS and
  answers in contracts. Each term is converted **at its own price**: `size` at
  `deal.avgPrice`, each `tpHistory` entry and each filled close order at the
  price it executed at, each `reduceFunds` entry at its own price, and the
  pending reduce-funds quote amount by plain division (on an inverse contract
  the quote amount IS the notional).

  `deal.avgPrice` is the contract-weighted average `calculateAbstractPosition`
  maintains and `getAvgPrice` (`dcaHelper.ts:7678-7702`) writes for every
  futures deal — verified on the deal above: `Σ cᵢpᵢ / Σ cᵢ = 77164.4…`, which
  is `deal.avgPrice` to the last stored digit.

  **Per-term conversion is load-bearing, not tidiness.** Netting in base first
  and converting the remainder once values a close at the ENTRY average
  instead of at the price it sold at. On this deal, a take-profit that has
  sold 400 of its 1158 contracts at `83041.7` is `0.4817` BTC, which read at
  `avgPrice` is 372 contracts — so the deal reads 29 contracts `under` when it
  is exactly covered. That was measured, not predicted: it is the fixture
  `COINM_PARTIAL` and the first draft of this fix failed it.

### 4.2 The actionable floor stays the venue's own

`isActionable` must keep answering "could an order be placed for this
difference at all". In contract space the venue's floor is one contract, which
is `quoteAsset.minAmount` of notional — so the call site passes
`price: contractSize` and leaves `baseMinAmount` / `quoteMinAmount` as the pair
states them. No new constant.

### 4.3 The log line names the unit it is counting in

The existing wording is `… more base than the deal owns`. On a contracts
comparison that sentence is the very confusion this defect is made of, so the
verdict text takes the unit as a parameter, defaulting to `base`.

### 4.4 `reconcileTpCoverage` stays pure and unit-agnostic

The conversion belongs at the call site. Nothing in
`tpCoverageReconcile.ts` learns what COIN-M is.

### 4.6 OKX / KuCoin futures compare in BASE, and only `origQty` moves

On `sizedInContracts` the contract is a FIXED multiple of base
(`getOKXDenominator`, `main.ts:8928`) with no price in it, so unlike §4.1 base
IS conserved and nothing about the tracked side has to change. The venue's
`origQty` is divided by the denominator — the exact inverse of the
multiplication `sendOrderToExchange` applied (`main.ts:7203`) and the same
division `convertOrderExecutedQty` already applies to this order's own
`executedQty` (`main.ts:7002`) — and every other term stays on the untouched
base path, `unit` included.

COIN-M takes precedence where a venue satisfies both predicates (`okxInverse`
carrying `settings.coinm`), because that is the precedence those two
conversions themselves use.

### 4.5 Nothing on this path regresses

`014`'s `unexplainedDrift` fee tolerance and `017`'s `under` re-arm branch are
recent fixes on these exact lines and must keep behaving identically for every
non-COIN-M deal. A non-COIN-M deal must take a numerically identical path.

## 5. Known limitation, stated on purpose

One term is still an approximation: `deal.size × deal.avgPrice` overstates the
contract count by the ladder's price dispersion (Cauchy–Schwarz:
`(Σcᵢ/pᵢ)(Σcᵢpᵢ) ≥ (Σcᵢ)²`). On the production deal that is `1158.36` against
a true `1158` — `0.031%`, a third of a contract, absorbed both by `014`'s fee
tolerance and by `4.2`'s one-contract floor. A much wider ladder could exceed
the fee band and read `under` by that fraction.
The alternative — re-deriving the position notional from the filled entry
orders, as `getTPOrder` does at `dcaHelper.ts:15188-15214` — is exact but is a
second definition of the position, which is precisely what `014` warns
produces a permanent false-drift family. The bias direction is safe: it can
only report `under`, whose repair (`placeOrders` re-sizing against `getTPOrder`,
which computes the contract count exactly) is a no-op when the resting order is
already right.

## 6. Out of scope — ledger note F326, left open

`trackedPosition` returning a NEGATIVE position on deal
`6a9668d68032d96fc7387dc5` (HFT-USDC) is a **different defect**, and this spec
neither fixes nor moves it. Read from production:

* `exchange: coinbase`, `settings.futures: false`, `settings.coinm: false` — a
  SPOT deal. Neither contract family applies, so it takes a path this change
  leaves numerically identical (pinned by the `AIXBTUSDT` case in §4.5).
* `size: 423360.49`, which is the position that REMAINS. `tpHistory` sums to
  `660 298.57` and `reduceFunds` to `300 786.81`, and `trackedPosition`
  subtracts both from it again — hence `-475 224.19`, and a drift printed as
  roughly twice the position.
* A likely mechanism worth starting from, not verified here: two of the three
  `tpHistory` entries are `D-TPR-` reduce-funds closes, and
  `filledCloseOrders` excludes orders carrying a `reduceFundsId`
  (`dcaHelper.ts`), so the id-dedup in `trackedPosition` cannot match them —
  and `reduceFundsBase` then subtracts the same quantity a second time.

Left open deliberately: it is a different subtraction, on a different venue
family, with its own failure direction (`over`, potentially large enough to be
actionable), and it deserves its own reproduction rather than riding a unit
fix.
