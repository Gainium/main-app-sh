# 111 — A part-filled LIMIT base entry opens the deal and rests its remainder

Feature spec, landing in existing code. It completes spec `103` §4.3 for the
bots it left short: a DCA bot whose deals start with a LIMIT order and whose
"Enter Market Timeout" switch is off (spec `100`). Those bots never enter at
market on their own. When their base order stops part-filled, the engine
settles it and opens the deal on what filled (spec `038`/`103` §4.3). That
protects the filled part with a take profit and stop loss, but the rest of the
base order is simply dropped.

## §1 Definition

### §1.1 Expected

- **§1.1.1** The deal still opens on what filled, exactly as today (spec `103`
  §4.3), so its take profit and stop loss cover that part at once.
- **§1.1.2** The unfilled remainder of the base order stays on the book as a
  LIMIT order at the price a LIMIT entry would use now (the latest price,
  rounded to the pair's price precision). When it fills, the fill joins the
  deal's position: average price, take profit and usage are recomputed.
- **§1.1.3** While the remainder rests, if the price moves the remainder is
  cancelled and placed again as a LIMIT at the new price. At an unchanged
  price it is left alone (the spec `103` §4.1 rule, applied to the remainder).
- **§1.1.4** The user can buy the resting remainder at market, on request
  only. That cancels the resting remainder, books whatever it had filled, and
  buys the rest with a MARKET order that joins the deal's position.
- **§1.1.5** The dashboard can tell a deal with a resting remainder apart and
  show how much of the base order has filled out of the total.
- **§1.1.6** Unchanged: a bot of this kind never sends a MARKET order on its
  own. Only §1.1.4, which the user triggers, does.

### §1.2 Actual (before)

- **§1.2.1** `settlePartialBaseEntry` cancels the remainder and opens the deal
  on the filled part. For these bots `topUpSettledBaseEntry` does nothing
  (spec `103` §4.4), and nothing re-places the remainder. The deal runs on a
  fraction of the base order the user configured, and its safety orders are
  sized for the full one.

## §2 Evidence

- Production, 2026-09-24 13:31:57Z, a live Coinbase bot (LIMIT entry, switch
  off, repositioning on): `base order ... stopped at 4.1 of 341.2 and nothing
  else will check it. Cancelling the remainder and opening the deal on what
  filled`. The deal's only other order was a take profit for 4 units.
- The same log line, fleet-wide, on one account's six bots: about 7 a day
  before spec `103` shipped (2026-09-24 05:27Z), about 1.4 an hour after it.
  Spec `103` §4.3 made the reposition tick settle these entries within about
  10 s instead of at the next bot restart.
- The existing harness case `§1.2.2/§4.3 switch off: a part-filled entry is
  settled by the tick` (`baseEntryReposition.harness.spec.ts`) shows the
  behaviour: the remainder is cancelled, the deal opens on 3000 of 3657.86,
  and nothing else is placed.

## §3 Design decisions

- **§3.1 The remainder is a pending add-funds entry.** The engine already has
  a resting LIMIT order that joins an open deal's position when it fills: the
  user's limit "add funds". It already has everything the remainder needs:
  - its own order row (`dealRegular`, stamped `addFundsId`);
  - `deal.pendingAddFunds` as its record, counted in `initialBalances` and so
    in usage while it rests;
  - re-sending when its order is gone after a reload (spec `110`);
  - cancellation with the deal's other orders on close/cancel
    (`cancelAllOrder`);
  - a fill path that updates `funds`, the average price, the take profit and
    the fee ledger.
  So the remainder is placed through `addDealFunds`, with two extra fields on
  its pending entry: `baseRemainder: true`, and `baseTotal` (the base order's
  requested quantity).
- **§3.2 It is placed when the deal opens.** It is placed from `startDeal`,
  after the deal is `open`, because `addDealFunds` only works on an open deal.
  The trigger is the base row the deal opens on. Any path that opens a deal on
  a short LIMIT entry for these bots gets a remainder: the tick settle, the
  restore settle, a venue-ended row, and a cancel that raced a partial fill.
- **§3.3 Spec `104` (the parked #803 change) is not folded in.** Spec 104
  kept a part-filled entry resting at an unchanged price WITHOUT opening the
  deal. For these bots the reposition window never closes (spec `100`), so on
  a quiet pair a filled part could go on with no take profit or stop loss
  indefinitely. That contradicts §1.1.1. Its intent, no churn at an unchanged
  price, is kept by §1.1.3 on the remainder row.

## §4 Required behaviour

- **§4.1 Rest the remainder.** When `startDeal` opens a DCA deal (not combo)
  from a base row with `executedQty < origQty`, and the bot may not enter at
  market (`startOrderType` LIMIT and enter-market timeout `0`), the remainder
  `origQty − executedQty` is placed:
  - rounded to the pair's base precision. Both quantities sit on the pair's
    step, so this only strips float noise (`341.2 − 4.1` is
    `337.09999999999997`); rounding down would drop a whole step;
  - as a LIMIT add-funds order at the latest price rounded to the price
    precision;
  - with `baseRemainder: true` and `baseTotal = origQty` on its pending entry;
  - with a `Deal` info event on the deal saying the rest is waiting as a limit
    order, so the extra pending order is explained before any dashboard shows
    it.
  None of this happens:
  - for a bot that may enter at market (its settle already tops up at market,
    spec `057`);
  - for a remainder below the venue's minimum (logged, nothing is placed);
  - for a deal that did not stay `open` (for example a terminal "simple" deal).
- **§4.1.1 Coin-margined and contract-sized accounts.** The remainder is
  placed there too (§4.1), once both quantities are in one unit:
  - the requested size is the base row's `origQty` as stored when it was
    placed. The row held in memory is not used for it: a reconcile rebuilds
    that row from the venue's answer, whose `origQty` is a contract count;
  - coin-margined (inverse) accounts, except Bitget, count in contracts,
    because base is not conserved on an inverse contract. The requested
    contracts are `max(1, round(origQty × origPrice / contractSize))`, what
    `sendOrderToExchange` sent. The filled contracts are
    `round(executedQty × price / contractSize)`, the inverse of
    `convertOrderExecutedQty`. The remainder, in contracts, is converted back
    to base at the price it is placed at, so the venue receives that many
    contracts. Production: 3075 of 5000 contracts on an ETHUSD inverse bot
    (`1.14357755 of 1.85947571` ETH) rests 1925 contracts;
  - OKX and KuCoin futures already carry `executedQty` in base, so the
    remainder is `origQty − executedQty` in base, as on every other account.
    Production: `25 of 243` on an OKX USDT-margined swap rests 218;
  - only for a base row the engine settled short itself (spec `103` §4.3,
    spec `059`). A row the venue reported `FILLED` while it reads short is
    not trusted there: on these accounts that is the signature of a
    quantity read in the wrong unit, and resting a "remainder" on it could
    double a position that is already whole;
  - a case that cannot be placed safely (a stored row that cannot be read,
    no price, a `FILLED` row reading short) is not dropped silently: the bot
    warns and the deal gets a `Deal` info event saying the rest was not
    placed and why. A difference that rounds to nothing (a whole
    coin-margined fill, whose base figures differ by rounding) says nothing.
  - the reposition tick (§4.3) and the buy at market (§4.6) take the
    remainder's requested size from its pending entry on these accounts, for
    the same reconcile reason.
- **§4.2 No MARKET order on its own.** Nothing in §4.1, §4.3 or §4.4 sends a
  MARKET order.
- **§4.3 Reposition the remainder.** While repositioning is on (a timeout
  above 0 and `notUseLimitReposition` off), a per-deal tick runs every
  reposition timeout:
  - **§4.3.1** It stops, without re-arming, when the deal is not `open`, or
    when it has no `baseRemainder` entry.
  - **§4.3.2** If the resting remainder order is at the rounded latest price
    (whether `NEW` or part-filled), it is kept and the tick re-armed.
  - **§4.3.3** If the price has moved, the order is cancelled. Any fill the
    venue reports, or that the engine had already seen when the cancel answer
    omits it (spec `059`), is booked into the deal through the add-funds fill
    path. The old entry is removed, and the unfilled rest is placed again per
    §4.1 at the new price with the same `baseTotal`.
  - **§4.3.4** If the cancel gets no terminal answer, nothing is re-placed,
    because the order may still rest. The tick is re-armed.
  - **§4.3.5** When no order is resting for the entry yet, the tick is
    re-armed and nothing is sent. The reload path (spec `110`) owns re-sending
    a missing entry.
- **§4.4 Restore.** On bot restore, every open deal with a `baseRemainder`
  entry gets the §4.3 tick armed, on the service-restart path as well. On a
  reload that re-sends pending funds (spec `110`), the re-sent entry keeps
  `baseRemainder` and `baseTotal`.
- **§4.5 Close and cancel.** Nothing new. The remainder is a `dealRegular`
  order of the deal, so it is cancelled with the deal's other orders, and the
  §4.3 tick stops at §4.3.1. Bot stop clears the tick timers.
- **§4.6 Buy the rest at market (user request).** The engine method
  `buyBaseEntryRemainder(botId, dealId)` runs on the deal's open
  `baseRemainder` entry. It stops the tick and cancels the resting order,
  booking its fills as in §4.3.3. It then removes the entry and sends the
  unfilled rest as a MARKET add-funds order. That order's fill path merges it
  into the position and recomputes the take profit and usage. If the cancel
  gets no terminal answer, nothing is bought, an error is reported, and the
  tick is re-armed. A deal with no remainder entry reports an error.
- **§4.7 Contract (additive only, Danger List #3).**
  - The GraphQL type `pendingAddFunds` gains `baseRemainder: Boolean` and
    `baseTotal: String`.
  - The new mutation `buyDealBaseRemainder(input: buyDealBaseRemainderInput!)`
    (`botId`, `dealId`) returns `cancelTerminalDealOrderResponse`, like
    `cancelPendingAddFundsDealOrder`.
  - The Mongo `funds` sub-schema gains the same two optional fields.
  - No existing field, name or payload changes.
- **§4.8 Unchanged.**
  - Bots that may enter at market (the spec `057` top-up).
  - Combo bots.
  - Spot and USDT-margined accounts that are not contract-sized: §4.1.1
    does not apply to them.
  - The `settlePartialBaseEntry` log line and settle.
  - The user's own add-funds orders, which carry neither new field.

## §5 Open questions

- **§5.1** A resting remainder counts as one pending add-funds level in
  `levels.all`, and its fill as one `funds` entry and one completed level, as
  every add-funds order does today. It is not relabelled as part of the base
  order in the level counters.
- **§5.2** A remainder rejected by the venue at placement is not retried
  outside the reload re-send. The venue's refusal is reported the way any
  add-funds refusal is.
