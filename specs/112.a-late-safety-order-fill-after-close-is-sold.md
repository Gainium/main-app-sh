# 112 — Every safety order that fills after its deal closed is sold

Bug spec.

## §1 Definition

### §1.1 Expected

- **§1.1.1** When a DCA deal closes (take profit filled) while fills of its
  own safety orders are still in flight, each of those fills adds coin the
  closed deal no longer tracks. Every such fill is sold back at market
  (`D-SR`), one sale per filled order, so the deal ends holding nothing.
- **§1.1.2** Unchanged: the deal-level remainder sale (what is left of the
  position at close, or a base order that filled after its deal was cancelled
  or closed) still runs at most once per deal, guarded by the deal's
  `sellRemainder` flag.
- **§1.1.3** Unchanged: a late fill of a closing-side order (a reduce-funds
  take profit, a multi-TP target) is never sold "again" through this path in a
  way it was not before.

### §1.2 Actual (before)

- **§1.2.1** `updateDeal` hands each late entry-side fill to
  `sellRemainder(dealId, executedQty, price, false, findDeal)`. `sellRemainder`
  refuses any call once the deal's `sellRemainder` flag is set, and the first
  sale of the deal — usually the close-time remainder sale — sets it in the
  database. As soon as the closed deal leaves memory, every later call reads
  the deal from the database, finds the flag, and returns "already sold". Only
  the fills handled while the closed deal was still in memory get sold. The
  rest of the coin stays in the account, unaccounted for by the deal.

## §2 Evidence

- **§2.1** Production (paper, Kraken spot, a BGB/USD DCA deal). One price tape
  moved through the take profit and five safety orders within the same
  millisecond. The engine logged, in this order: one safety-order fill
  (`Regular order FILLED`), `TP order FILLED`, then four more safety-order fills
  (3.83173, 4.06452, 4.31413 and 4.57722 base). The deal closed on the TP.
  Two `D-SR` market sells followed: 3.60014 (the close-time remainder) and
  4.57264 (= 4.57722 × (1 − 0.1 % taker), the first late fill). The other three
  late fills, 12.21 base in total, were never sold. The pair's venue minimums
  (2.5 base / 0.5 quote) do not refuse any of them, so the minimum check did
  not skip them.
- **§2.2** The first late fill was handled while the closed deal was still in
  memory, where the flag is never set (`sellRemainder` only mirrors it onto an
  OPEN deal). `processDealClose` then drops the deal from memory, and from that
  point the flag is read from the database. The tests in
  `lateEntryFillAfterClose.spec.ts` reproduce both halves.

## §3 Assumption (root cause, hypothesis)

- **§3.1** The per-deal latch conflates two different things: "the deal's own
  remainder was sold" and "this particular late fill was sold". Late
  entry-side fills are distinct quantities. They are already deduplicated per
  `clientOrderId` by `updateDeal`'s `dealUpdateOrders` set, which is never
  cleared for the life of the process, so the deal latch is not what prevents
  a double sale of one order. It only blocks the second and later orders.

## §4 Fix

- **§4.1** `sellRemainder` takes a `lateEntryFill` flag. When it is set, the
  deal-level latch is not consulted. Everything else (minimums, rounding,
  balances, profit, writing the latch) is unchanged.
- **§4.2** `updateDeal`'s closed-deal branch sets it only for
  `typeOrder === dealRegular` (safety orders and add-funds fills, the
  entry side). Every other caller and order type keeps today's behaviour.
