# 037 — Close-price ladders repeat the zero-price laundering

Bug spec. Follow-on to `035.deal-close-price-is-laundered-from-zero-into-a-giveaway`,
which fixed the same defect on one of the three places `getTPOrder` derives a
close price.

## 1. Definition

`getTPOrder` builds a deal's closing orders. It derives a close price in three
structurally identical places:

1. the single close order (`tpPrice`),
2. each rung of the take-profit ladder (`settings.useMultiTp`),
3. each rung of the stop-loss ladder (`settings.useMultiSl`).

**Expected.** All three refuse to produce an order when the price they derive
from is unusable, meet the venue's notional floor by moving *quantity*, and are
compared against the live market before being returned.

**Actual.** Only (1) does. Spec `035` hardened the single order; (2) and (3)
each still carry a verbatim copy of the pre-`035` sequence:

```
price = avgPrice * (1 +/- target%) * displacement    -> 0 when avgPrice is 0
if (price === avgPrice) price = one tick             -> 0 === 0 satisfies it
(no zero guard)
if (price * qty < quoteAsset.minAmount)
   price = ceil(minAmount / qty)                     -> a tradeable wrong price
```

and spec `035`'s market-deviation guard is positioned **above** both ladder
blocks, so no ladder order has ever been compared to the market.

### 1.1 Why `035`'s guard does not cover the ladders

`035` guards `tpPrice`. The ladders do not use `tpPrice` — each rung
re-derives its own price from `avgPrice`, ignoring the caller-supplied `price`
argument that `tpPrice` honours. So a call that supplies an explicit close
price (or a configured fixed take-profit price) keeps `tpPrice` healthy, passes
`035`'s guard, and reaches a ladder in which **every rung is 0**.

The production call site for a ladder stop-loss does exactly this: it passes
the requested close price as both the market reference and the explicit `price`
argument, with `sl: true`.

## 2. Evidence

### 2.1 The original defect's arithmetic, confirmed

Every deal-close SELL in the reported burst was priced at exactly
`ceil(quoteAsset.minAmount / qty)` — 7 of 7 reproduced to the digit from the
recorded quantity alone. The ratio between that price and the deal's average
entry is simply the position's notional value, which is why the ratios spread
from ~9x to ~130x with no other pattern.

### 2.2 The ladders reproduce it end-to-end

`src/bot/dca/tpLadderPriceRefusal.harness.spec.ts` drives the real
`getTPOrder`. Against the source as shipped by spec `035`:

| case | result |
|---|---|
| TP ladder, zero `avgPrice`, valid `price` argument | `1000000 @ 0.000001` — a SELL of 1,000,000 units of a 51,029-unit position, priced 2561x under the market |
| SL ladder, same | identical |
| TP ladder, healthy prices, rung under the notional floor | rung repriced from the derived `0.002599` to `0.003334` (`ceil(1/300)`), 28% above the real take-profit |
| TP ladder priced far below a live market | returned, never compared |

### 2.3 The reported post-deploy recurrence is not one

The two orders cited as a recurrence are a resting LIMIT take-profit followed
~20s later by a MARKET close order on the same symbol, on deals whose average
entry sits far above the current market. The LIMIT price is correctly derived
from `avgPrice`; the MARKET order's recorded price is the live market. The
"collapse" is the difference between those two, not a mispricing. The same pair
appears on those deals 155 times each, going back a month before the deploy.

Re-running the original defect's signature — a close order priced far *below*
its own deal's average entry — over the same period returns 17 orders, all on
the day of the report, and **none** afterwards.

## 3. Assumption

*Hypothesis, not fact.* The root cause is duplication: the close-price
derivation exists three times, and a fix applied to one copy leaves the other
two intact. The guards must be applied to the ladder branches, and the
market-deviation check must move to where it can see every order actually being
returned.

Gate 2's tests must fail *because* the ladder branches lack those guards — not
because the call is refused earlier for some other reason. Two of the six
deliberately pin healthy behaviour and are expected to pass before and after.

## 4. Required behaviour

- **§4.1** A ladder rung whose price is not finite and positive is refused —
  before the one-tick tie-break nudge can convert it into a tick. A rung that
  cannot be priced refuses the whole ladder, not just that rung: a ladder
  missing a rung is not a smaller close plan, it is a wrong one.
- **§4.2** A ladder rung meets the venue's notional floor by moving
  **quantity**, never price, and never sells more than the deal holds.
- **§4.3** Every order actually returned — single or ladder — is refused if it
  is priced far enough from the live market to give value away. One-sided: only
  the value-losing direction. An unknown market fails open.
- **§4.4** A deal with real prices is untouched, to the unit.

## 5. Out of scope

- `comboHelper.ts` carries a similar notional-floor block. It was not traced
  for this issue and no evidence implicates it.
- The three deals in §2.3 re-issue a failing market close every few hours and
  never complete. That is a real defect and a separate one.
