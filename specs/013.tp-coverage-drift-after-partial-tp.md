# 013 — A deal's resting take-profit no longer covers the position it tracks

Issue #696, follow-up to #694 (`006.dca-tp-replace-partially-filled`,
`007.tp-resize-cancel-must-never-promote`).

## 1. Definition

A DCA deal that is `open` holds a base position and arms exactly one resting
take-profit for it (`useMultiTp: false`). The invariant the engine intends is:

> **§1.1** The base quantity the deal's live take-profit orders can still sell
> equals the base quantity the deal still holds.

Written out, with `R` the *resting* coverage and `P` the *tracked* position:

    R = Σ over live dealTP orders of (origQty − executedQty)
    P = |deal.size| − (base this deal has already closed)
    healthy  ⟺  R = P

**Expected.** After a safety order fills, the engine cancels the resting
take-profit and re-arms a larger one, so `R` returns to `P` — that is what
`placeOrders` exists to do, and what #694 restored.

**Actual.** For deals that took a *partial* take-profit fill before #694
shipped, `R ≠ P` and stays that way. Two shapes:

- **§1.2 under-covered (`R < P`).** The stale `PARTIALLY_FILLED` take-profit
  still rests and still locks its unfilled remainder, so the replacement cannot
  be funded and is rejected. Part of the position has no take-profit at all.
- **§1.3 over-covered (`R > P`).** The wallet *could* fund the replacement, so
  the replacement was placed **on top of** the still-live stale order. The deal
  now has two live take-profits selling more base than it owns.

Neither shape self-heals: nothing re-examines coverage on a timer. The deal is
only revisited when its next safety order fills, which is a market event that
may never arrive.

**§1.4** The correction is the same for both shapes: cancel the stale
`PARTIALLY_FILLED` take-profit **without** promoting it to `FILLED`, then re-arm
through `placeOrders`.

**§1.4.1** Re-arming is only safe when nothing healthy is left resting to
duplicate. It is therefore correct in exactly two situations: something was
cancelled, or there was no live take-profit to begin with. An over-covered deal
whose live take-profits are *all* `NEW` has no residue from this defect, and
cancelling a healthy resting order to fix it is a different decision with a
different risk — such a deal is reported and left alone.

**§1.5** A drift smaller than the venue's own minimum order size is not
actionable — no order could be placed for it — and must be read as covered.

**§1.6** A take-profit that is `PARTIALLY_FILLED` is **not** by itself a defect.
The common and healthy case is a partial fill whose remainder still exactly
covers the rest of the position. Only a drift in `R` vs `P` is the defect.

**§1.7** The venue's live order state, not the local DB, decides what is
resting. The `orders` collection retains long-dead `NEW` rows.

## 2. Evidence

Read-only `mongo` against production, 2026-09-06 (VPS triage key).

**§2.1** `db.orders.find({typeOrder:'dealTP', status:'PARTIALLY_FILLED'})` →
**18** orders. Computing `R` and `P` per deal over that population:

| deal | symbol | user | P (tracked) | R (resting) | drift |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `6a90e161a7` | B3-USDC | `6a8b1db8…` | 935,356 | 824,863 | **−110,493** |
| `6a978104aa` | CTSIUSDT | `674b015f…` | 533 | 848 | **+315** |
| `691de676b6` | DGBUSDT | `638e2be9…` | 31,773.5 | 52,419.9 | **+20,646.4** |
| 13 others | — | — | — | — | **0** |

Three deals, three distinct users. The other 13 partially-filled take-profits
have `drift = 0` — they are healthy, and confirm §1.6: partial-fill alone is not
the signal.

**§2.2 — the under-covered deal, `6a90e161a76e7fe63ea3118f` (B3-USDC).**
`size = 989,459`, `tpHistory = [{qty: 54,103}]` → `P = 935,356`. Its one live
take-profit `D-TP-TNTUXFh6ohYEWhi6dK8cYPufQgEYqj` is `PARTIALLY_FILLED`
`54,103 / 878,966` → `R = 824,863`. **110,493 B3 has no take-profit.** This
reproduces the reported numbers exactly. The single re-place attempt was
rejected `free 110,493 / required 934,119` — the free balance being precisely
the uncovered remainder, because the stale order holds the rest.

**§2.3 — the over-covered deal, `6a978104aa99d06351d63e3a` (CTSIUSDT).**
`size = 900`, `tpHistory = [{qty: 367}]` → `P = 533`. Two live take-profits:
`TP-Jjsrx…` `PARTIALLY_FILLED 367/682` (rests 315) **and** `TP-qWLCK…` `NEW 533`
(rests 533) → `R = 848`. The `NEW` order is a correctly-sized replacement — it
equals `P` exactly — placed on top of the stale one, which is pure residue.
`315` CTSI of over-sell would be eaten out of the user's other holdings.

**§2.4 — a third, unreported deal, `691de676b60a5e1cf2d420eb` (DGBUSDT)**, same
shape as §2.3: stale `PARTIALLY_FILLED` resting 20,689.5 alongside a correct
`NEW` 31,730.4 against `P = 31,773.5`.

**§2.5 — the DB is not the venue.** Grouping *all* live `dealTP` rows by deal
over the whole collection reports deals with tens of thousands of "live"
take-profits (`69e04b567a…` DOGSUSDT: 43,760 rows, `R` 43,000× `P`) and would
flag 781 deals as over-covered. Those rows are dead residue the reconcile never
wrote back. A sweep that trusts the DB is unusable; §1.7 is not optional.

## 3. Assumption

The root cause of the *residue* is the #694 defect, already fixed: `placeOrders`
looked the resting take-profit up with `status: 'NEW'` only, so a
`PARTIALLY_FILLED` one was invisible and was never cancelled before the
replacement was sent.

The root cause of the *persistence* — what this spec addresses — is that *the
engine has no periodic check of §1.1 at all*. Coverage is only ever established
as a side effect of `placeOrders`, which runs on an order-fill event. So a deal
whose coverage drifted while that path was broken stays drifted after the path
is fixed, because nothing re-runs it. The fix for #694 is necessary and not
sufficient: it stops new drift, and cannot repair existing drift.

**Falsifiable form:** given a deal whose live take-profits do not sum to its
tracked position, no code path in the DCA engine re-arms the take-profit until
`processFilledOrder` fires for one of that deal's safety orders.

## 4. Scope

**§4.1** Detection is always on and side-effect-free: compute the verdict from
venue-confirmed orders during the reconcile pass and log it.

**§4.2** Correction cancels and places **real orders** and is therefore
**disarmed by default**, behind an explicit operator-set environment flag. It
must never run as a consequence of deploying this change.

**§4.3** Out of scope: `useMultiTp` deals, whose several take-profit levels have
their own completion accounting (`multiTpCompletion`), and non-DCA strategies.
Also out of scope, and for the same reason — the deal is *correct* without a
resting take-profit — are trailing take-profit deals, deals that do not close on
take-profit, and deals whose take-profit is managed externally (`externalTp`).

**§4.4** A deal holding **no** live take-profit at all is out of scope. Every
deal passes through that state between its base order filling and its
take-profit being armed, so reading it as uncovered would report a large part of
the fleet on every pass. The defect in §1.2/§1.3 always leaves at least the
stale order resting, so nothing in scope is lost. The decision function still
answers for the empty case; the *caller* declines to ask.

**§4.5** The drift is a **standing condition** in the sense of spec 008: it does
not change between reconcile passes, and only this check or a safety-order fill
will move it. Reporting it per pass would put one warning per drifted deal per
pass into the fleet log — the exact shape spec 008 exists to prevent. The report
goes through `ConditionLatch`, keyed by deal, and clears when coverage returns
so a recurrence is reported afresh. The correction rides the same latch, so an
armed engine that cannot repair a deal retries on that cadence instead of
cancelling and re-placing orders on every pass.
